Band gap Eg = eV

1. What is a semiconductor?

A material whose electrical conductivity sits between a conductor and an insulator, and can be changed on purpose by doping, heat, light, or an electric field.

Conductivity

Resistivity about 10⁻³ to 10⁸ Ω·cm. Metals are near 10⁻⁶; insulators above 10⁸.

Temperature

Resistance falls as temperature rises (negative temperature coefficient), the opposite of metals.

Doping

Adding about 1 impurity atom per 10⁷ host atoms can raise conductivity by a factor of 1000 or more.

Light and fields

Photons create electron-hole pairs (photoconductivity); a gate voltage can switch current on and off.

Key concepts

Valence band
Highest band filled with electrons at 0 K.
Conduction band
Empty band above it where electrons move freely.
Band gap (Eg)
Energy needed to lift an electron across. Si 1.12 eV, GaAs 1.42 eV.
Electron-hole pair
A freed electron leaves a positive “hole” that also carries current.
Fermi level
Energy with 50% occupation probability; sits mid-gap in intrinsic material.
Mobility and lifetime
How fast carriers drift per unit field, and how long they survive before recombining.

Timeline

1833 Faraday sees silver sulfide conductivity rise with heat.
1874 Braun discovers rectification at a metal-sulfide contact.
1947 Bardeen, Brattain and Shockley build the point-contact transistor at Bell Labs (Nobel Prize 1956).
1958 Jack Kilby builds the first integrated circuit (Nobel Prize 2000).
1971 Intel 4004: about 2,300 transistors. Modern chips hold tens of billions.

2. Band theory of solids

When atoms form a crystal, their energy levels merge into bands. What the top bands look like decides the material’s type.

Conductor

Bands overlap or the top band is half full. Eg ≈ 0. Example: Cu, Al.

Semiconductor

Small gap, about 0.1 to 3 eV. Thermal energy kT ≈ 0.026 eV at 300 K excites a few carriers. Example: Si, Ge.

Insulator

Large gap above about 4 eV. No carriers at room temperature. Example: diamond, SiO₂.

Carrier density follows nᵢ ∝ T^(3/2) · exp(−Eg / 2kT). That is why the hero demo shows more electrons when Eg is small or T is high. Select “Copper” in the lab to see overlapping bands.

Direct vs indirect gap: in direct materials (GaAs, GaN) an electron can drop to the valence band and emit a photon, so they make LEDs and lasers. In indirect materials (Si, Ge) the transition also needs a phonon, so they emit little light but are cheap and easy to process.

5. The P-N junction

Join p-type and n-type material and a depletion region forms. Change the bias and see what happens.

Diode equation: I = I₀ (e^(qV/kT) − 1). Silicon diodes start conducting near 0.7 V; Ge near 0.3 V.

7. From sand to chip

Purify Quartz sand (SiO₂) is reduced to metallurgical silicon, then refined to 99.9999999% (“nine nines”) electronic-grade silicon.
Grow The Czochralski method pulls a single-crystal ingot from molten silicon on a rotating seed.
Slice The ingot is sawn into wafers (up to 300 mm), then polished to a mirror finish.
Pattern Photolithography transfers circuit patterns onto photoresist; EUV light (13.5 nm) prints the smallest features.
Dope Ion implantation or diffusion places boron, phosphorus or arsenic in exact regions.
Build up Oxidation, CVD, sputtering and etching add insulators and copper wiring layer by layer.
Package The wafer is diced, each die is tested, wired and sealed.
